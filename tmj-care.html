TMJ & jaw care

Jaw comfort, taken seriously.

Attention to jaw discomfort, clicking, limited opening and facial pain, starting with understanding where it comes from.

Why this page exists

Jaw discomfort deserves its own conversation.

Jaw pain is easy to live around. People adjust how they chew, avoid certain foods, and assume it is simply how things are now.

It is worth describing properly to someone who will listen. Understanding where discomfort is coming from is the part that tends to get skipped, and it is the part this practice gives time to.

Public patient feedback about Dr. Andrews mentions his attention to TMJ-related care specifically, which is why it has a page of its own here rather than a line in a list.

What people describe

Signs people often bring in.

If any of these sound familiar, they are worth raising. None of them on its own means anything in particular, and none is a diagnosis.

Jaw pain or tenderness

Aching around the joint, or soreness that is worse at certain times of day.

Clicking or popping

Noise from the joint when you open, close or chew.

Limited opening

A jaw that will not open as far as it used to, or that catches.

Facial discomfort

Pain spreading through the face, temples or around the ear.

Morning tightness

Waking with a tight or tired jaw.

Discomfort when chewing

Finding certain foods harder than they used to be.

General information only. This page describes the kinds of concerns people raise. It is not medical or dental advice, makes no diagnosis, and promises no particular outcome. Specific treatments, appliances, timescales and costs are deliberately not listed, because none of that has been confirmed with the practice.

What to expect

A conversation before anything else.

Describe it

What you notice, when it started, and what makes it better or worse.

Be examined

An assessment of how the jaw moves and where the discomfort sits.

Understand the options

An explanation of the findings and what can reasonably be done about them.

Tell us about the jaw discomfort.

Describe it in general terms when you get in touch. The detail is best discussed in person.

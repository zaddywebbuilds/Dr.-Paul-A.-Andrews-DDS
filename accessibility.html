Accessibility

Built to be usable.

What has been done, and what is honestly still outstanding.

Request an Appointment Privacy Call the Office

What has been built in

  • Semantic landmarks, a single h1 per page and an ordered heading structure.
  • A “skip to content” link as the first focusable element on every page.
  • Visible focus outlines on every link, button and form control.
  • Full keyboard operation, including the FAQ accordion and the mobile menu, which traps focus while open and closes on Esc.
  • Descriptive alternative text on meaningful images, and empty alt on decorative ones.
  • Form labels tied to their controls, with status messages announced politely.
  • Respect for the operating system's reduced-motion setting: floating, pulsing and reveal animations stop.
  • Text that reflows without horizontal scrolling down to 320px.
  • Tap targets sized for touch on small screens.

Known limitations

  • The design uses translucent glass surfaces over photography. Contrast has been kept conservative, but a full audit against WCAG 2.2 AA has not been carried out on every state.
  • The embedded map is third-party content and its accessibility is outside this site's control. A text address and a directions link are always provided alongside it.
  • Testing with assistive technology has not yet been performed by a user of that technology.

Before launch. A full accessibility audit is recommended, and this statement should be updated with the standard the practice is committing to, the date of the audit, and a contact route for access problems.

Telling us about a problem

If any part of this site is difficult to use, please get in touch through the contact page or call the office, and describe what you were trying to do.
